Notes

The setup. With a single database, a business operation is one ACID transaction. When data spreads across shards or microservices, there's no global coordinator, so a single logical operation now spans separate local transactions. That's a distributed transaction — it needs a strategy for "all commit, or clean up on failure."

2PC gives strong consistency but is blocking: if the coordinator crashes after collecting votes, participants stay locked. Practical only inside distributed databases (Spanner, CockroachDB) — don't build 2PC across services yourself.

Sagas drop all-or-nothing in exchange for non-blocking behavior. A chain of local transactions, each committing and releasing immediately; if a later step fails, run business-level compensations to undo earlier ones. Two flavors: choreography (events + subscribers, fine up to ~5 steps) and orchestration (Temporal, AWS Step Functions, scales further).

Transactional outbox fixes the dual-write problem: write the business row and the event into the same DB transaction, then a background relay (CDC or polling) reliably publishes from the outbox.
